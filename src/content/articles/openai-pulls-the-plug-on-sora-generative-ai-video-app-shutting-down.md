OpenAI is officially sunsetting Sora, its highly anticipated generative AI application built to transform text prompts into high-quality video content. At the time of the announcement, the tech giant did not disclose a specific reason for the abrupt closure of the platform.

## Bidding Farewell to Creators

The Sora development team broke the news on March 25 via a [statement on X](https://x.com/soraofficialapp/status/2036546752535470382), expressing deep gratitude to their user base. 

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet"><p lang="en" dir="ltr">We’re saying goodbye to the Sora app. To everyone who created with Sora, shared it, and built community around it: thank you. What you made with Sora mattered, and we know this news is disappointing.<br><br>We’ll share more soon, including timelines for the app and API and details on…</p>&mdash; Sora (@soraofficialapp) <a href="https://twitter.com/soraofficialapp/status/2036546752535470382?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw">March 24, 2026</a></blockquote> <script async src="https://platform.twitter.com/widgets.js" charset="utf-8"></script>

While the immediate shutdown announcement has caught many off guard, OpenAI has promised to release further operational details shortly. This upcoming guidance is expected to outline the exact timeline for deprecating the app and its API, as well as provide instructions for users looking to export and preserve their generated video content before the servers go offline.

## The Disney Deal and Industry Shifts

The decision to shutter Sora marks a sharp strategic pivot for OpenAI, especially following the major [licensing agreement struck with Disney in December 2025](https://openai.com/index/disney-sora-agreement/). That milestone partnership was slated to allow Sora users to incorporate iconic Disney characters into their AI-generated videos, a feature that generated massive industry buzz.

In the wake of the shutdown, a Disney representative addressed the shifting landscape in a [statement to Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/openai-shutting-down-sora-video-disney-1236698277/). Highlighting the rapid evolution of the AI sector, the spokesperson noted that Disney respects OpenAI's decision to exit the video generation business and shift its priorities elsewhere. 

"We appreciate the constructive collaboration between our teams and what we learned from it," the Disney spokesperson said, adding that the entertainment giant remains committed to engaging with emerging AI platforms responsibly, while simultaneously protecting intellectual property and creator rights.
